# Skribbl.io wins on zero friction, not polish

skribbl.io is a free, account-less browser Pictionary: one player picks a secret word and draws it on a shared 800×600 canvas, while everyone else races to type it into a chat box that doubles as the guess field. Faster guesses earn more points, and the drawer earns points for every player who gets it. That loop has barely changed since a solo developer, "ticedev", released it around March 2017. The one big overhaul, the "Fresh paint" redesign of 9 November 2022, added mobile support, undo, a 26-colour palette, votekick, and up to 20 players in private rooms. The game still draws an estimated **7–9 million visits a month with roughly 11-minute sessions**, funded by ads and boosted by the 2020 lockdowns. Its appeal is social rather than mechanical: a link-based private room, no install, and the comedy of bad drawings under time pressure. Its weaknesses are just as clear. Public lobbies suffer trolls and auto-guess cheats, the scoring underpays the drawer, and moderation is thin. Rivals that broke out did so by changing who knows the word, what earns points, or when the payoff lands, as Gartic Phone's telephone chain and Jackbox's bluffing games did. For an original pixel-art game on PeerJS with 2–8 players, this points to a few priorities. Copy the proven turn structure, keep the word on the host only, pay the drawer fairly, and add small-group modes the classic race handles badly. Look nothing like skribbl, whose current art already uses chunky, low-resolution doodles.

A note on evidence. skribbl.io's server code is closed, so its scoring formula, hint timing, close-guess threshold and votekick threshold are not public. The rules below come from mirrored copies of the real client, a reverse-engineered protocol write-up, and actively maintained third-party libraries. Unverified values are labelled as such. Live in-game screenshots of the current UI could not be captured, because creating a room was not permitted from the research session. Gartic Phone and Gartic.io returned HTTP 403, so the competitor visuals here are limited to Drawasaurus. Several popularity and sentiment findings come from search-result summaries rather than full page reads.

## An eight-state server loop turns one drawer into a timed race

### The turn cycle: choose, draw, reveal, repeat

The server runs the game as an explicit state machine with eight states: 0 waiting for players, 1 game starting, 2 "Round N" splash, 3 drawer picking a word, 4 drawing, 5 per-turn results, 6 final podium, and 7 private-lobby setup ([Skribbler constants.js](https://github.com/Skribblers/skribbler/blob/HEAD/src/constants.js); [protocol gist](https://gist.github.com/MrDiamond64/b2081f2cb4ca6d11e848edaeb5ae1814)). Every state packet carries the seconds remaining. Real captured packets show a **2-second round splash and a 15-second word choice**. If the drawer does not pick in time, the server auto-selects a word ([protocol gist](https://gist.github.com/MrDiamond64/b2081f2cb4ca6d11e848edaeb5ae1814)). Only the drawer receives the word options; everyone else sees "$ is choosing a word!". A game needs **at least two players** to start ([client game.js](https://github.com/ElijahKasler/website/blob/HEAD/projects/skribbl/js/game.js)). Guides cite **80 seconds as the standard draw time** ([CrazyGames](https://www.crazygames.com/game/skribblio)). The official About text sums up the structure: "A normal game consists of a few rounds, where every round a player has to draw their chosen word and others have to guess it". The five-step tutorial adds the one hard rule, "No spelling!" ([Zpayer index.html](https://github.com/Zpayer/Skribbl.io-Source-Code/blob/HEAD/index.html), a copy of the real page). The state flow, with one "Round N" splash followed by repeated choose/draw/results cycles, fits the common description that each player draws once per round. No primary source documents the turn order or how mid-round joiners are handled.

A turn ends in one of four ways, each announced above "The word was '$'": **everyone guessed, time is up, the drawer left, or the drawer got skipped** ([client game.js](https://github.com/ElijahKasler/website/blob/HEAD/projects/skribbl/js/game.js)). The results overlay lists each player's points gained that turn ("+N", with zeros greyed out) and plays a success or failure sting. The final podium fills slots 1–3, **tied players share a slot**, and chat announces "$ won with a score of $!". In private rooms, scores then reset and the room returns to the setup screen rather than kicking anyone out ([Skribbler Client.js](https://github.com/Skribblers/skribbler/blob/HEAD/src/client/Client.js)). The client adds tension with sound: a ticking clock and a wobbling clock icon in the **last 10 seconds of drawing and last 4 seconds of word choice** ([client game.js](https://github.com/ElijahKasler/website/blob/HEAD/projects/skribbl/js/game.js)). Late joiners get a lobby-data packet containing the current state, time left, revealed hints and **every draw command so far**, so their canvas can be rebuilt exactly ([protocol gist](https://gist.github.com/MrDiamond64/b2081f2cb4ca6d11e848edaeb5ae1814)).

### Hints reveal random letters into underscore slots

Guessers never receive the word, only an array of word lengths such as `[5]` or `[5,3]`. The client renders one underscore slot per character, including the spaces between words, followed by a small length label like "5 3" ([client game.js](https://github.com/ElijahKasler/website/blob/HEAD/projects/skribbl/js/game.js)). During the turn the server sends hints as `[position, letter]` pairs, and each revealed letter pops in orange with a bounce animation ([protocol gist](https://gist.github.com/MrDiamond64/b2081f2cb4ca6d11e848edaeb5ae1814)). Spaces and "+" separators come pre-revealed ([Skribbler Client.js](https://github.com/Skribblers/skribbler/blob/HEAD/src/client/Client.js)). The Hints setting runs from **0 to 5, and 0 disables hints entirely** ([Skribbler constants.js](https://github.com/Skribblers/skribbler/blob/HEAD/src/constants.js)). Two alternative word modes exist. **Hidden** shows "???" and "WORD HIDDEN" with no length information. **Combination** makes the drawer pick two words from two separate choice screens, to be guessed as a compound ([client game.js](https://github.com/ElijahKasler/website/blob/HEAD/projects/skribbl/js/game.js)). Once you guess correctly, your own client fills in the whole word, while others learn only that you got it. When hints are revealed during the turn is decided on the server and has not been documented.

### Scoring rewards speed, and the drawer's formula is opaque

No exact formula is public; the server simply sends `[playerId, newTotal, pointsAdded]` triplets ([client game.js](https://github.com/ElijahKasler/website/blob/HEAD/projects/skribbl/js/game.js)). Every secondary source agrees on the shape. **Guessers score more the earlier they guess.** The drawer "gets points based on how many players successfully guess the phrase and the amount of time left when the last person guesses" ([mechanicsofmagic](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2024/04/24/critical-play-scribbl-io-2/)). **If nobody guesses, nobody scores** ([CrazyGames](https://www.crazygames.com/game/skribblio)). One article fitted a model of roughly 100–500 points scaled by time remaining, but it is explicitly an estimate, not the real code ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/board_it_b11ff7d58bf863f8/games-like-skribbl-what-they-share-and-what-to-look-for-in-a-table-19h3)). One mechanic compounds the speed reward: **the server cuts the remaining time after a correct guess** ([protocol gist](https://gist.github.com/MrDiamond64/b2081f2cb4ca6d11e848edaeb5ae1814)). Later guessers therefore score less both because they are later and because the clock got shorter. The turn ends immediately once everyone has guessed.

### Chat is the guess box, and it hides spoilers by design

The chat input is capped at **100 characters** and reads "Type your guess here..." ([Zpayer index.html](https://github.com/Zpayer/Skribbl.io-Source-Code/blob/HEAD/index.html)). A correct guess is never broadcast as text. Everyone sees a green "X guessed the word!", hears a chime, and watches that player's row turn green. The best idea in the chat design is the **guessed-players channel**. Once you have guessed, your messages reach only the drawer and other correct guessers, shown on a pale-green background, so people still guessing never see them ([client game.js](https://github.com/ElijahKasler/website/blob/HEAD/projects/skribbl/js/game.js)). Near misses trigger a private "'housr' is close!" in yellow ([protocol gist](https://gist.github.com/MrDiamond64/b2081f2cb4ca6d11e848edaeb5ae1814)). The exact threshold is unknown, though the example implies an edit distance of about one character. Rapid-fire messages trigger "Spam detected! You're sending messages too quickly." Profanity filtering is a client-side toggle, on by default. Chat lines also pop up as speech bubbles beside the sender's avatar for 1.5 seconds. Viewers can thumbs-up or thumbs-down the current drawing, which is announced in chat but appears to have no effect on score ([client game.js](https://github.com/ElijahKasler/website/blob/HEAD/projects/skribbl/js/game.js)).

## Eight host settings, custom words and a five-button toolbar define the toolkit

### Private rooms expose every knob; public rooms expose none

Private rooms are shared by invite link (`skribbl.io/?<id>`). The link is hidden until hovered, a small courtesy to streamers. Only the room owner can change the eight server-enforced settings ([Skribbler constants.js](https://github.com/Skribblers/skribbler/blob/HEAD/src/constants.js); [Zpayer index.html](https://github.com/Zpayer/Skribbl.io-Source-Code/blob/HEAD/index.html)). Public rooms have no owner and use fixed settings, with players matched by language or picked from a room browser ([protocol gist](https://gist.github.com/MrDiamond64/b2081f2cb4ca6d11e848edaeb5ae1814)). Default values beyond the 80-second draw time are unverified.

| Setting | Allowed values |
|---|---|
| Language | 28 languages (English, German, … Tagalog, Turkish) |
| Players | 2–20 |
| Draw time | 15, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70, 80, 90, 100, 120, 150, 180, 210, 240 s |
| Rounds | 2–10 |
| Game mode | Normal, Hidden, Combination |
| Word count (choices offered to drawer) | 1–5 |
| Hints | 0–5 |
| Custom words | Comma-separated, **min 10 words, 1–32 chars each**, plus a "Use custom words only" checkbox |

Custom words go to the server as a single comma-separated string, and multi-word entries like "candy cane" are allowed ([protocol gist](https://gist.github.com/MrDiamond64/b2081f2cb4ca6d11e848edaeb5ae1814)). With **fewer than 10 words the server ignores the list** ([Skribbler Client.js](https://github.com/Skribblers/skribbler/blob/HEAD/src/client/Client.js)). The overall length cap reads 20,000 characters in one client copy and 10,000 in another, so it was probably raised at some point ([Zpayer index.html](https://github.com/Zpayer/Skribbl.io-Source-Code/blob/HEAD/index.html); [client game.js](https://github.com/ElijahKasler/website/blob/HEAD/projects/skribbl/js/game.js)). Before 2022, private rooms held at most 12 players and custom lists needed only four words ([mytour.vn](https://mytour.vn/en/blog/kinh-nghiem-hay/how-to-create-a-private-room-in-skribblio-mytour.html)).

### Moderation is votekick plus owner powers

The 2022 update gave every player **Votekick, Mute and Report** buttons. Report offers three reasons: inappropriate messages or drawings, spam, and botting or cheating. Room owners can **Kick and Ban** directly ([Zpayer index.html](https://github.com/Zpayer/Skribbl.io-Source-Code/blob/HEAD/index.html); [protocol gist](https://gist.github.com/MrDiamond64/b2081f2cb4ca6d11e848edaeb5ae1814)). Votekick progress shows in chat as "$ is voting to kick $ (1/2)". The owner cannot be votekicked, and kicked players get a rejoin cooldown and eventually a "kicked too many times" lockout ([client game.js](https://github.com/ElijahKasler/website/blob/HEAD/projects/skribbl/js/game.js)). The required vote count is set by the server; reviewers describe it as "half" or "more than half" of the lobby, and the two accounts conflict ([mechanicsofmagic](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2024/04/24/critical-play-scribbl-io-2/)). If the owner leaves, ownership passes to another player rather than the room dying ([protocol gist](https://gist.github.com/MrDiamond64/b2081f2cb4ca6d11e848edaeb5ae1814)). Mute is purely client-side.

### Drawing tools: brush, fill, undo, clear, and nothing else

The current toolbar is deliberately sparse. It has **Brush (B), Fill (F), Undo (U), Clear (C)** and a Swap key (S) for trading primary and secondary colours. Hotkeys can be rebound. There is **no eraser** (you paint white), and there are no line, shape or text tools ([client game.js](https://github.com/ElijahKasler/website/blob/HEAD/projects/skribbl/js/game.js)). Brush sizes are **4, 10, 20, 32 and 40 px**. The palette has **26 colours in two rows of 13**, light shades over their dark partners. Left-click paints the primary colour and right-click the secondary ([Skribbler constants.js](https://github.com/Skribblers/skribbler/blob/HEAD/src/constants.js); [skribbltypo default-palettes.ts](https://github.com/toobeeh/skribbltypo/blob/HEAD/src/app/features/drawing-color-palettes/default-palettes.ts)). Pen devices get optional pressure sensitivity. The pre-2022 toolbar visibly had a 22-colour (11×2) palette, an eraser, four sizes and no undo ([galehouse5 screenshot](https://github.com/galehouse5/skribbl-io-autodraw/tree/master/screenshots)).

### Stroke sync is compact, index-based and replayable

skribbl.io's sync design maps almost directly onto a PeerJS architecture. Each draw command is a tiny integer array. A line is `[0, colorIndex, size, x1, y1, x2, y2]` and a fill is `[1, colorIndex, x, y]`. Colours travel as **palette indices, not RGB** ([protocol gist](https://gist.github.com/MrDiamond64/b2081f2cb4ca6d11e848edaeb5ae1814); [Skribbler DrawBuilder.js](https://github.com/Skribblers/skribbler/blob/HEAD/src/builders/DrawBuilder.js)). The drawer samples the pointer at up to 90 Hz and flushes unsent commands **every 50 ms in batches of at most 8**; the server drops oversized packets ([client game.js](https://github.com/ElijahKasler/website/blob/HEAD/projects/skribbl/js/game.js); [Zpayer constants.js](https://github.com/Zpayer/Skribbl.io-Source-Code/blob/HEAD/src/core/constants.js)). Undo records the command count at each pointer-up as a stroke boundary and broadcasts "roll back to index N", so every client replays the log up to that point. Lines are rasterised as hard-edged, non-antialiased circles written straight into pixel data. Every client's canvas therefore comes out identical pixel for pixel, and flood fill never leaks through soft edges. Only the drawer may draw, and both client and server enforce this ([Skribbler Canvas.js](https://github.com/Skribblers/skribbler/blob/HEAD/src/client/Canvas.js)).

## Royal blue, rainbow markers and blob avatars make the look unmistakable

### Layout: logo, game bar, then players | canvas | chat

The in-game screen is a CSS grid. A logo sits on top, then a full-width bar with the timer, "Round N of M" and the word. Below that are three columns, **players | canvas | chat**, with the toolbar under the canvas ([style.css](https://github.com/Zpayer/Skribbl.io-Source-Code/blob/main/public/css/style.css)). On portrait phones the layout stacks: bar, canvas, tools, then players and chat side by side, then the input. The word bar reads "DRAW THIS" with the full word for the drawer, or "GUESS THIS" with underscore slots for everyone else. The timer is a number inside an alarm-clock drawing. Word choice, turn results and the podium all appear as overlays on the canvas itself. The arrangement is the same as before the redesign, as the pre-2022 captures below show.

### Colour, type and shape: school-blue page, marker accents, near-square chrome

The page background is **#124DA5** tiled with a darker-blue doodle wallpaper (suns, houses, fish, bikes, skulls). Panels are translucent royal blue, `rgba(12,44,150,0.75)`, bordered in near-black navy #040A33 ([style.css](https://github.com/Zpayer/Skribbl.io-Source-Code/blob/main/public/css/style.css)). Game surfaces are white or light grey with zebra-striped rows. Feedback colours carry meaning:

| Colour | Use |
|---|---|
| green #56CE27 / #5BDD4A | correct guess and guessed player rows |
| yellow #E2CB00 | "is close" |
| blue #3975CE | "is drawing now" |
| orange-red #CE4F0A | leaves and kicks |
| orange #FFA844 | owner, and revealed hint letters |
| purple #AB66EB | active tool or size |

The buttons are a green Play! (#53E236), a blue normal button (#2C90E8) and an amber "danger" votekick button, not red ([skribbltypo colors.ts](https://github.com/toobeeh/skribbltypo/blob/main/src/util/typo/themes/colors.ts)). The font is **Nunito**, a rounded sans-serif at weights 400–800. The chrome is almost square, with a **3px border radius and hard 3px offset drop shadows with no blur**, which gives it a slightly retro web-1.0 feel. A dark theme ("Turn the lights off") and seasonal Halloween and snow skins exist ([style.css](https://github.com/Zpayer/Skribbl.io-Source-Code/blob/main/public/css/style.css)). The logo is a hand-lettered rainbow wordmark, each letter a different saturated colour with a thick black outline, ending in an orange pencil.

### Avatars and art style: layered doodle blobs with a 1% surprise

An avatar is four stacked sprite layers: **body colour (28 options, 10 of them hatched), eyes (57), mouth (51)** and an optional "special" accessory such as a wizard hat, pirate hat or Santa hat. Each layer is picked with arrows. The randomise die has a **1% chance of adding a rare accessory** ([client game.js](https://github.com/ElijahKasler/website/blob/HEAD/projects/skribbl/js/game.js)). Atlases use 48-px cells. A crown marks the room owner, and on the podium the winner wears the crown and does a 3.8-second squash-and-stretch hop in front of a trophy ([style.css](https://github.com/Zpayer/Skribbl.io-Source-Code/blob/main/public/css/style.css)). The art throughout (icons, logo, wallpaper, the chalky "How to play" doodles) is **thick-outlined, flat-filled marker drawing**. The 2026 captures show it is now low-resolution and upscaled with hard pixel edges. For a "pixel-art" competitor, that matters: skribbl already sits close to that aesthetic.

## Zero-install rooms drove 7–9 million monthly visits on a free, ad-funded model

skribbl.io's growth came from friction removal, not marketing. You need no account and no download, just a link. During the 2020 lockdowns workplaces adopted it: one health-care start-up had **about 100 employees playing weekly** as "a way for employees to connect" ([Charlatan](https://charlatan.ca/?p=100073)). A reviewer called it "probably the online party game that I've played the most over quarantine" ([Study Breaks](https://studybreaks.com/tvfilm/online-party-games)). The demand has not faded. Semrush estimated **7.16M visits in November 2025 with an average session of 11:26** ([Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/website/skribbl.io/overview/)), and about **9.3M visits in its March and July 2026 snapshots** ([Semrush competitors](https://nl.semrush.com/website/skribbl.io/competitors)). An 11-minute average is long for a browser game. It suggests that once a group forms, it plays several full games. skribbl.io is also the most consistently named drawing game in "browser games to play with friends" lists, typically praised because you just "open a private room and send the link" ([FRVR](https://frvr.com/blog/guides/best-browser-games-to-play-with-friends/); [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/best-browser-games); [GameSpew, Oct 2026](https://www.gamespew.com/2026/10/5-free-multiplayer-browser-games-to-play-with-friends/)). "Unblocked at school" extensions point to heavy student use, which raises the stakes for word-list and chat safety ([Chrome Web Store](https://chrome.google.com/webstore/detail/skribblio-unblocked-at-sc/dkgpifieloncldkhkfbhpcchdanmhpde/support?hl=en)).

The fun itself is well understood. Reviewers locate it in "laughing at your friends' attempts to draw things under pressure" ([Tabletop Gaming](https://www.pressreader.com/uk/tabletop-gaming/20210625/282376927542830)) and in the vulnerability of drawing badly in public, which essays frame as "bluffing, judging and getting vulnerable" ([Critical Play](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2022/04/15/critical-play-bluffing-judging-and-getting-vulnerable-skribbl-io-tristan-wang/)). One design essay argues that the absence of penalties for wrong guesses is load-bearing. Punishing misses would kill the good-faith guessing that generates the laughs ([mechanicsofmagic](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/?p=26690)).

On money, skribbl.io is free. Every source points to an ad-supported model with **no accounts, no cosmetics and no premium tier**. It is also syndicated on portals such as CrazyGames, which typically share ad revenue ([CrazyGames](https://www.crazygames.com/game/skribblio)). The site's footer carries a "Privacy Settings" link, consistent with ad-consent tooling, though no ad rendered in the 2026 captures, so the exact formats remain unverified. Revenue figures have never been published. Peers follow the same pattern. Drawasaurus runs a full-height ad column asking visitors to "Disable ad block to support Drawasaurus" (see below). Gartic Phone shows a single ad before a round ([WhistleOut](https://www.whistleout.com/CellPhones/Apps/gartic-phone-app-review)). Only Jackbox charges up front for its drawing games. Many unofficial "Skribbl/Scribble" apps clutter the mobile stores, with unclear ties to the original ([Apptopia](https://apptopia.com/google-play/app/com.Skribbl.io/about)), a sign of unmet demand for a native mobile experience.

## Trolls, cheaters and a stingy drawer score top the complaint list

### Public lobbies are where the experience breaks

The documented complaints focus on strangers. Critical Play analyses describe players who disconnect or refuse to draw, and conclude that the "lack of robust moderation in open rooms allows such negative behaviors to go unchecked" ([mechanicsofmagic PDF](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Skribbl-1.pdf)). One essayist voted alone to kick cheaters while chat turned hostile, and proposed a higher minimum age for public play ([Critical Play 2024](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2024/04/24/critical-play-scribbl-io-2/)). Community posts track racial slurs spread through shared rooms and custom word lists ([itch.io](https://itch.io/post/11256421)), showing how user-supplied words can be weaponised.

### Cheating is an industry because the word space is knowable

The Chrome Web Store hosts several helpers. One matches the hint pattern against "3600+ words". Another, "Skribbl Auto Guesser", auto-types guesses on a delay tuned to dodge spam kicks ([Skribbl.io Helper](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/skribblio-helper/pnfncicapicbmndoelcfebipoibdackb); [Skribbl Auto Guesser](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/fjnaelnpipplicnabjfdilmncaehfldf)). Greasy Fork carries auto-guess scripts updated as recently as 2026 ([Greasy Fork](https://greasyfork.org/en/scripts/by-site/skribbl.io)). Auto-draw bots are marketed too. These tools work because the built-in word list is finite and public, the length pattern is visible, and hints narrow the search further. The November 2022 word-list expansion briefly broke helper dictionaries ([Chrome Web Store](https://chrome.google.com/webstore/detail/skribblio-cheats/idphhmfinajlkohjcpkcblhddeccokpk)). Helpers stop working entirely on custom words in private rooms ([Greasy Fork](https://greasyfork.org/scripts/407408)). That points to the real defence: unpredictable word pools.

### Scoring punishes the person doing the hardest job

Several analyses found the drawer earning **less than the first guesser, and sometimes less than the last**. That "does not provide an incentive for the drawer to draw well". Players also learn to spam guesses because being first pays most ([Critical Play 2022](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2022/04/15/critical-play-bluffing-judging-and-getting-vulnerable-skribbl-io-tristan-wang/); [Critical Play: Judging](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/?p=27382)). The essays propose several fixes. Guessers could rate the drawer to award drawer points. A drawer who gets everyone to guess could earn as much as the fastest guesser. The drawer could earn zero if nobody guesses, and skill-based matching could separate players.

### What players reach for when the game falls short

No official wishlist exists, so the best signal is what the redesign, extensions and clones added. The redesign itself added **undo, more colours and mobile support**, implying these were the loudest gaps. Extensions add word solvers, custom lists and themes. Clones such as Sketchful advertise larger rooms and tablet support ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/sketchful-io)). Gartic Phone's success shows appetite for a different loop entirely ([dinogame.gg](https://dinogame.gg/blog/games-like-skribbl-io/)). Several often-repeated complaints could not be sourced: writing the word on the canvas, obscure words, lag, ad annoyance and the small mobile canvas. The same goes for requests like replays, galleries and shape tools. Treat these as plausible but unverified.

## Rivals change who knows the word, what earns points, and when the payoff lands

The genre splits into four families, and the breakout hits came from changing the rules rather than polishing the race. The live race-to-guess clones (Sketchful, Drawasaurus, Drawize, Gartic.io) "copy the loop almost exactly" ([dinogame.gg](https://dinogame.gg/blog/games-like-skribbl-io/)). The games people remember changed the structure.

The well-loved twists all make **bad art funny instead of losing**: the telephone degradation, the convincing lie, the remixed slogan. Gartic Phone's rise was streaming-driven, with one 2021 session passing **2.8M views** ([Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/editorials/what-is-gartic-phone-and-why-do-streamers-love-it-the-multiplayer-drawing-game-explained)). Drawful is "better when the crowd is bigger" ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/switch-eshop/drawful_2)), while Draw Something proved a game can work with exactly two people if it is co-operative and replay-based.

Pixel constraints are rare but promising. An itch.io pixel-budget prototype drew praise for "mak[ing] the shapes so distinct with such limited pixels" ([itch.io](https://itch.io/post/10732114)). The constraint swaps drawing skill for abstraction skill, which flattens the gap between artists and non-artists. Jackbox sets the accessibility bar with extended and no-timer options, family-friendly mode, profanity filters, host moderation and subtitles ([Jackbox](https://jackboxgames.com/blog/accessibility-features-in-the-jackbox-party-pack-10)).

## Conclusion

skribbl.io shows that a draw-and-guess game does not need depth to last. It needs instant entry, a legible 80-second loop, and a few precise social affordances. The most important of those are the guessed-players chat channel, the private "close!" nudge and the green row flip. Most of what players dislike traces to two root causes. The first is that strangers in public rooms bring trolls, slurs and poor moderation. The second is that a finite, publicly known word space combined with first-guess-wins scoring enables cheats and spam guessing while underpaying the drawer. A friends-only PeerJS game sidesteps the first problem by architecture. It can fix the second with game design: unpredictable word pools, plus scoring that rewards communication over reflexes.

The deeper lesson from the competitors is that the classic race scales badly at the bottom of a 2–8 range. With one or two guessers there is barely a race at all. Gartic Phone, Drawful and Draw Something each won by changing who knows the word, what earns points or when the payoff lands. A pixel grid suits those twists unusually well. A grid cell is a natural sync event, a replay frame and an animation frame, so stroke replay, reveal races, memory-copy chains and flipbooks become cheap to build rather than stretch goals.

## Design implications for our game

| Area | Recommendation | Grounding |
|---|---|---|
| Core loop | Keep the proven state machine: lobby → round splash → word choice (≈15 s, auto-pick on timeout) → draw (preset 60/80/120 s) → reveal with +N deltas → podium with shared ties. End turns on all-guessed, timeout, drawer-left or drawer-skipped. | skribbl's eight states and four end reasons |
| Host authority | Only the host holds the word. Send guessers word lengths and `[pos, letter]` hints only, and validate guesses on the host. Never put the word in the guesser DOM. Accept that the host client knows the word, which is tolerable among friends. Consider rotating host duties or excluding the host's guesses in competitive play. | Helper and auto-guess cheats; TASVideos DOM-reading thread |
| Sync | Send draw ops as palette indices plus integer grid cells, in small batches (~50 ms). Keep a per-turn op log so late joiners and reconnectors rebuild the canvas. Implement undo as "roll back to op N". The deterministic pixel grid makes every peer identical by construction. | skribbl's command format, 8-op/50 ms batching, late-join log |
| Host loss | PeerJS rooms die with the host unless you plan otherwise. Mirror skribbl's ownership transfer with a host-migration snapshot (state, scores, op log), or at least allow a fast reconnect to the same room code. | Owner-transfer packet |
| Scoring | Guesser points = f(time left) × order multiplier, with no penalty for wrong guesses. Drawer points scale with the share of players who guessed, capped near the top guesser's score, and are zero if nobody guesses. Optionally add likes as a small drawer bonus. | Critical Play complaints about the underpaid drawer |
| Chat | Combined guess/chat box (~100 chars). Never echo correct guesses. Use a guessed-only channel, a private "close!" message at edit distance 1–2, case- and accent-insensitive matching, and a spam limiter. | skribbl chat design |
| Words | Curated themed packs with difficulty tiers, a custom list (minimum ~10 words), and a profanity filter on custom words and names that defaults on. Large or rotating pools blunt solvers. | Slur complaints; 3,600-word solver lists |
| Small groups | Add modes for 2–4 players, such as a reveal race (cell-by-cell replay, earliest guess wins), co-op relay, pixel telephone or memory copy, and same-prompt showdown with voting. | Draw Something, Gartic Phone, Pictionary All Play, Pixelate |
| Tools | Use a fixed low-res 4:3 grid (e.g. 32×24 or 48×36, with 16×16 as a "hard" option), nearest-neighbour scaling, pencil, fill, eraser, undo, clear, two to three brush sizes, line and rectangle tools, and a curated 16–24 colour palette in light-over-dark pairs. Make it tap-friendly on phones. | skribbl's sparse toolbar; Pixelary grid |
| Moderation | In friends' rooms: host kick and ban, a votekick for the host-less case, client mute, a "skip drawing" control, and a family-safe default. | skribbl 2022 moderation; Jackbox VIP tools |
| Look | Differ on every signature element. Avoid royal blue, the rainbow outlined wordmark, the doodle wallpaper, round blob avatars and a purple active-tool highlight. skribbl's art is already chunky and pixel-like, so the distinction has to come from palette (e.g. warm teal, plum or cream paper), a pixel typeface instead of Nunito, an original avatar silhouette and layer set, and a distinct backdrop. | Visual analysis and 2026 screenshots |
| Payoff | End-of-game gallery and replay or GIF export of each pixel drawing. This "laugh at the drawings" moment is something skribbl lacks. | Gartic Phone's reveal-driven appeal |
| Money | A static site has no server costs beyond hosting and signalling. Keep core play free. If monetising, use unobtrusive ads or optional cosmetic avatar parts and word packs, never paywalled modes. | skribbl, Gartic and Drawasaurus ad models |
