# Mount Veil: story draft

An original story for the fan game, built like Celeste's: a climber, the part of herself she's
running from, people with their own problems met on the way, a false victory, a fall, making
peace and a real summit. The characters, plot, secret and dialogue are all new.

---

## The idea in one paragraph

**Wren Calder**, 22, arrives at Mount Veil eight months after her older sister **Isla** died.
The two of them always planned to climb it together "someday." Wren tells everyone she's
climbing it *for* Isla. The truth is she's climbing to stay busy enough that she never has to
feel anything. Mount Veil has a reputation: *it shows you whatever you carried up with you.*
What Wren carried is **the Echo**, a copy of her made from every word she has swallowed since
the funeral. The Echo repeats what Wren says, but honestly. Wren says "I'm fine." The Echo
says "I'm not."

**The core secret, revealed slowly:** the night Isla died, she called Wren, and Wren let it go
to voicemail because they'd argued about something small. Wren has never played the message.
She's sure it proves Isla died thinking they were still fighting.

---

## Main characters

**Wren Calder (the climber).** Stubborn, funny, always moving. She cracks jokes to change the
subject. When she feels grief she turns it into motion: *if I stop, I sink.* Her arc goes
from running, to stopping, to letting herself feel it. She carries Isla's **red hairclip**
(her hair gets it in the art).

**The Echo (her shadow self).** Looks like Wren, but pale and blurred, as if seen through
fog. She isn't evil; she's everything Wren refused to say. At first she seems like a monster
because she's loud where Wren is quiet. She can't lie, which is why Wren is afraid of her.
Her arc: from being chased away, to locked away, to accepted. When they make peace, Wren
gets her **second dash** for good. That's the story reason for the double dash.

**Ottilie "Tilly" Marsh (the mapmaker).** 70s, sharp-tongued, lives in the trailhead cabin.
She's spent 40 years mapping Mount Veil, and the map is never right because the mountain
changes for each climber. She gives Wren a **blank map** that fills in as she climbs (the
chapter select screen). She tried the summit three times as a young woman and turned back
each time. She hides it behind jokes, much like Wren does.
*Role:* a mentor who talks by radio and lantern signals. Her big line in the finale:
*"I didn't turn back because I was weak. I turned back because I hadn't brought the right
things up with me yet."*

**Pascal Ferro (the clockmaker's son).** 19, anxious and overly polite, the last person
living in Clockwork City, a mining town the mountain turned into machinery. His father went up
the mountain 12 years ago and never came back, and Pascal has kept every clock in the city
wound since, "so it's the right time when he gets home."
*He is Wren's mirror image:* she can't stop moving, and he can't start. She's cruel to him in
Chapter 4, he surprises her later, and they become real friends.

**Lumen (the keeper of Dream Hollow).** A soft, moth-like spirit that collects the dreams
people abandon on the mountain. It isn't a villain; it truly wants people to be happy, and it
thinks that means never waking up. It offers Wren a perfect dream in which Isla is alive.

**Isla Calder (the sister).** Never seen alive. We meet her through Wren's memories, things
she left behind, and finally her voice.

## Chapter by chapter

The five chapters already in the game are marked **(built)**. The rest match the planned
Chapters 6–9 and post-game chapters.

### Prologue: The Bridge *(new short intro)*
Night. Wren crosses a broken rope bridge to the trailhead, cracking jokes to herself out loud.
A lantern swings on: Tilly, on her porch. *"Nobody comes up here at midnight unless they're
running from something."* Wren: *"I'm running TO something."* Tilly: *"Mm. Mountain'll decide
that."*
*Gameplay:* teaches running, jumping and climbing. The dash is unlocked at the end, when Wren
leaps the last gap.

### Chapter 1: The Foothills *(built)*
Wren climbs happily and too fast. Strawberries grow wild here, and Tilly asks her to bring
some back "if she's still herself on the way down" (the collectibles get a story reason).
**End scene:** Wren camps. In the dark she tells Isla's hairclip about her day, the way she
used to call her sister. Her reflection in the kettle keeps talking after she stops.

### Chapter 2: Windswept Ridge *(built: wind)*
The wind carries voices. Wren hears her own voice ahead of her on the ridge, finishing her
sentences wrong. **The Echo appears for the first time**, perched on a rock:
*Wren:* "Who are you?"
*The Echo:* "The part of you that's tired."
*Wren:* "I'm not tired."
*The Echo:* "I'm SO tired."
Wren runs, and the Echo follows her through the gale.
*New gameplay moment:* a **shadow chase room** where the Echo copies your exact path a second
behind you. It's the same idea as Badeline's chase, with our own character.

### Chapter 3: The False Summit *(built: pink crystals; renamed from "The Summit")*
Wren pushes through, sure the top is close. Pink crystals grow where "two hearts beat
nearby," and she can feel the Echo close behind. She reaches a peak with an old flag and
laughs: *she made it.* Then the fog lifts, and **the real summit is still far above.** This
was a false summit.
**End scene:** Wren's first real crack. She shouts at the sky that it's not fair. The Echo
doesn't mock her; she just sits a few metres away. Neither speaks. A radio crackles: Tilly,
from far below: *"Everybody hits that one. Sit a while."* Wren doesn't sit. She keeps going.

### Chapter 4: Clockwork City *(built: zip movers, winged strawberries)*
A town swallowed by gears. The zip movers are the old ore carts, still running their routes.
Wren meets **Pascal**, who treats her like his first visitor in years, which she is. He asks
her to restart the great Clock Tower "so the city is ready." The winged strawberries were once
the town's carrier birds.
**The fight:** Pascal admits who he's waiting for. Wren, raw from the false summit, snaps:
*"People who go up this mountain don't come back. You're winding clocks for a ghost."* She
hears herself and freezes. The Echo, quietly: *"You were talking to yourself just then."*
**End scene:** Wren restarts the Clock Tower alone. It chimes for the first time in 12 years.
Pascal doesn't come out to see it, and Wren leaves without apologising.

### Chapter 5: Dream Hollow *(built: dream blocks, switches)*
Worn out, Wren falls asleep at the edge of a starry cave and wakes *inside* the dream. The
dream blocks are soft pockets of memory. **Lumen** greets her kindly and offers her the house
she grew up in, with Isla at the kitchen table asking how the climb went. Wren wants to stay.
**The switches are "waking lights."** Each one is a true memory Wren has avoided, and she
has to light every one to open the gate out of the dream:
1. The argument with Isla (it was about a borrowed jacket).
2. The phone buzzing on the counter that night. Wren turning it face down.
3. The voicemail notification she has swiped away every day for eight months.
**End scene:** Wren lights the last one and wakes up gasping. The Echo is kneeling beside her,
no longer blurred, and says the thing Wren never let herself think: *"You think she died
angry at you."* Wren has no answer.

### Chapter 6: The Lantern Terraces *(planned: clouds, move blocks)*
Old rice-terrace fields full of drifting fog clouds and stone blocks that slide when pushed.
An uneasy truce: Wren lets the Echo walk beside her, as long as she stays quiet. **Pascal
catches up.** Wren's cruel words stuck with him, and for the first time in 12 years he left
the city. He's terrible at climbing and cheerful about it. Wren finally apologises, badly and
honestly, and he accepts. Tilly signals from below with lanterns spelling *PROUD*. Wren
pretends not to cry. The Echo cries enough for both of them.

### Chapter 7: The Hall of Stillness *(planned: swap blocks, red bubbles)*
A temple where time is stuck. It traps people inside the moment they can't let go of. Pascal
steps in and freezes inside a moment of waiting at his father's workbench, and Wren has to
carry his frozen lantern through the temple to free him. There she finds **his father's
climbing journal.** The last page shows he reached the summit and chose to keep travelling.
He never came back because he didn't want to, not because he died. Wren has to decide whether
to tell Pascal. *(She does, later, gently. His reaction lands in Chapter 9.)*
**The betrayal:** at the heart of the temple is a mirror that can seal a reflection away.
Wren traps the Echo in it: *"I can't finish this with you screaming in my ear."*

### Chapter 8: Undertow *(planned: feathers, bumpers; the low point)*
Without the Echo, Wren feels calm and light. Too light. On the next ridge she misjudges a jump
she'd have made easily before, and falls all the way down into the gorge at the bottom of
Mount Veil. She's hurt, alone, and it's night. Tilly's voice comes through the cracked radio,
and she finally tells the story of her three failed climbs.
Wren goes back up to the temple to free the Echo, and this time she listens. *The Echo:*
*"I'm not here to stop you. I'm the part of you that loved her enough for it to hurt. You
keep trying to leave me behind, and I keep being the only one who remembers her properly."*
They make peace, and Wren **gets her second dash** for good. The chapter ends with a
flight up out of the gorge on feathers, the Echo racing beside her rather than behind her.

### Chapter 9: The True Summit *(planned: everything remixed)*
The last climb uses every mechanic from the game. Pascal climbs part of the way, having
decided what to do with the truth about his father: he'll leave his own journal for whoever
comes next. Tilly talks Wren up the last wall by radio, at the height where she turned back 40
years ago.
**The summit:** sunrise. Wren sits with the Echo and finally **plays Isla's voicemail.** It's
ordinary: Isla laughing, saying keep the jacket, it looks better on her anyway, *"call me
back whenever, no rush, love you, you idiot."* She wasn't angry. She never was. Wren laughs
and sobs at once. She clips the red hairclip to the summit cairn. The Echo fades gently into
her, and her hair shows both colours for a moment.

### Epilogue: Tilly's Cabin
A warm scene with no platforming. Tilly finally finishes her map, with the summit drawn in
Wren's handwriting. Pascal shows up with a small clock he built for the cabin. The strawberry
count turns into a joke (Tilly makes tea from however many you brought back). Wren leaves a
voicemail for no one in particular, just saying out loud how she feels. The credits roll.

## Post-game chapters (like Celeste's Core and Farewell)

### Chapter 10: The Heart of Veil *(hot/cold switching)*
Weeks later, Wren and the Echo (now more of an inner voice than a separate body) go inside
the mountain to find out why it "shows people what they carry." The mountain's heart swings
between burning anger and frozen numbness, the two ways Wren has handled grief. You switch
between them to get through. Nobody tells a moral; you just play it.

### Chapter 11: Letters *(the very hard final chapter; paper lanterns to glide on)*
A year after the summit. Wren climbs a dreamlike version of the mountain made from Isla's
unsent letters, which she found while clearing out Isla's flat. It's the hardest chapter in
the game. At the end Wren writes Isla one last letter and lets it go with a paper lantern.
It's about the difference between getting over grief and carrying it well.

## How the story connects to the game

| Game feature | Story reason |
|---|---|
| Strawberries | Tilly asks for them; they become her tea in the epilogue |
| Double dash | Wren and the Echo making peace in Chapter 8 |
| Pink crystals (Ch. 3) | "Grow where two hearts beat": the Echo is near |
| Zip movers (Ch. 4) | The mining town's old ore carts |
| Winged strawberries | The town's old carrier birds, easily frightened |
| Dream blocks and switches (Ch. 5) | Soft memories, and the true memories that wake you |
| Chapter select | Tilly's map, filling in as you climb |
| Shadow chase rooms | The Echo following Wren in Chapters 2 and 3 |
| Deaths | Nobody in the story treats them as failure. Tilly: *"You fell. So what? You're still on the mountain."* |

## What I'd build for the story

1. **Dialogue boxes** with portraits (Wren, the Echo, Tilly, Pascal, Lumen). The text types
   out; press any button to speed it up or continue.
2. **Cutscenes** at chapter starts and ends, plus a few in the middle, with the camera and
   characters moving.
3. **The Echo chase** as a new room type.
4. **Settings:** skip cutscenes, story on/off (for speedrunners), and scenes you've seen
   saved in your save file.
5. **A Prologue** and **Epilogue** added around the current five chapters.

## Decisions for you

- **Names:** keep Wren, Isla, Tilly, Pascal, Lumen and the Echo, or rename any of them?
- **Renaming Chapter 3** from "The Summit" to "The False Summit": it sets up the story's
  first big twist. OK?
- **Tone:** this draft is emotional (grief, guilt), like Celeste. Do you want it this
  serious, or lighter?
- **Build order:** (a) add the story to the five existing chapters first, then build Chapters
  6–9 with their story, or (b) build everything chapter by chapter.
